planning your first battery-aware day ride

Range, climbs and chargers: a simple way to plan a day ride you'll actually finish.

The best first long ride on an e-bike isn't the most ambitious one. It's the one where you come home with battery to spare, a few good stops behind you and the feeling that next time you could go further. Here's a simple way to plan it.

start with an honest range

The number on the box is a best case. Real range depends on the assist level you actually use, your weight and luggage, the tyres and the weather. Set your range in Zyflow the way you really ride, not the way the brochure rides. If you're not sure, be a little conservative. Finishing with 20% left feels a lot better than finishing with 2%.

pick a rolling route first

A rolling countryside day is where almost every rider should begin. The rhythm is predictable, the climbs are short and you get a feel for how your battery behaves without the mountains making the decisions for you. Browse the routes by region and ride type, and choose one that matches the day you want, not the day you think you should manage.

read the battery plan before you leave

When you plan a ride, Zyflow builds a battery plan from the route's elevation, your range settings and the wind and weather forecast. It shows where the effort will be and where the chargers sit along the way.

  • Look for the tight section. The part of the ride with the hardest climb and the furthest charger is the one to plan around
  • Check the wind. A headwind on the way home can cost more than the climb on the way out
  • Mark a charging stop. Even if you don't need it, knowing it's there changes how you ride
"Finishing with 20% left feels a lot better than finishing with 2%."

plan the stops, not just the line

A good day ride is more than distance. Turn on Coffee and Restaurants in Explore and pick a place to stop around the halfway point. If it has a charger or bike parking, even better. Stops are where the ride becomes a day out, and they're easy to plan once you can see them.

then go a little further

After a ride or two you'll know how your bike behaves on climbs, in the cold and with a full bag. That's when longer days and bigger mountains start to make sense. Build up slowly, keep a charger in the plan, and let each ride teach you something for the next one.
